01 — Robarts Research Institute

A $150 microscope that can see malaria

Scroll to light the board

Fourier ptychography builds one sharp image out of many blurry ones, each lit from a different angle. Our three-person team built a 3D-printed version around a Raspberry Pi, aimed at malaria diagnosis where a lab microscope isn’t affordable. I owned the hardware and wrote the code that drives it, from the STM32 firmware to the Python library on the Pi.

Hardware

LED illumination board

Off-the-shelf LED matrices flickered even at full brightness, which ruins image capture. I designed a 2-layer board with about 150 digital (individually addressable) LEDs in a ring layout, an STM32 and 3.3 V to 5 V level shifting, sized to sit on top of the Pi. It went through three revisions.

Firmware

Firmware and control

The Pi sends commands to the STM32 over SPI: which LED to light, and its colour and brightness. The LEDs are digital, driven by a timing-critical data signal, so I wrote C firmware that bit-bangs that waveform from a GPIO pin, debugging it on a logic analyzer until the LEDs responded. An extra GPIO line tells the Pi when the requested LED is on, and my Python library waits for that signal before each image is captured.

Mechanical

Microscope body

I redesigned the 3D-printed body in Onshape over five-plus iterations to line up the lens, sensor and aperture, and worked out the aperture size so it takes in light from the outermost LEDs while blocking reflections from the rest of the board.

We imaged malaria parasites inside red blood cells, and later cut the parts cost to about $150 CAD. The work is part of a paper in Proc. SPIE (2024), where I’m a co-author.

02 — Queen’s capstone, team of four

A touchscreen that works inside an MRI scanner

A psychology lab at Queen’s wants people inside an MRI scanner to use a touchscreen during fMRI studies. Anything magnetic is a problem in there, so the tablet runs on battery and gets its video and touch data over fibre optics from the control room.

Magnetics

Air-core inductors

Ferrite-core inductors saturate in the scanner’s field, and nobody sells air-core parts at the values we need. I’m designing our own, sized with Wheeler’s formula.

Electronics

Display driver board

I’m replacing the ferrous components on a commercial display’s driver board with non-magnetic parts.

Mechanical

Enclosure

A 3D-printed case for the display, electronics and battery, with no metal fasteners or framing.

And one fix at home.

03 — Home, February 2025

Fixing my headphones

I snapped the cable on my favourite wired headphones. Inside were three very thin conductors. I soldered them back together. Heat shrink wouldn’t have been strong enough to protect wires that thin, so I designed and 3D printed a mold to keep the joints apart, filled it with resin, then sanded the result into a solid housing. They work again.
