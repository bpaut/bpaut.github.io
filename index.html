A $150 microscope that can see malaria

Fourier ptychography builds one sharp image out of many blurry ones, each lit from a different angle. Our three-person team built a 3D-printed version around a Raspberry Pi, aimed at malaria diagnosis where a lab microscope isn’t affordable. I owned the hardware.

LED illumination board
Off-the-shelf LED matrices flickered even at full brightness, which ruins image capture. I designed a 2-layer board with about 150 addressable LEDs in a ring layout, an STM32 and 3.3 V to 5 V level shifting, sized to sit on top of the Pi. It went through three revisions.
Firmware and control
C firmware on the STM32 and a Python library on the Pi talk both ways over SPI, setting LED patterns and brightness and stepping through lighting sequences in sync with the camera.
Microscope body
I redesigned the 3D-printed body in Onshape over five-plus iterations to line up the lens, sensor and aperture, and worked out the aperture size so it takes in light from the outermost LEDs while blocking reflections from the rest of the board.

We imaged malaria parasites inside red blood cells, and later cut the parts cost to about $150 CAD. The work is part of a paper in Proc. SPIE (2024), where I’m a co-author.

A touchscreen that works inside an MRI scanner

A psychology lab at Queen’s wants people inside an MRI scanner to use a touchscreen during fMRI studies. Anything magnetic is a problem in there, so the tablet runs on battery and gets its video and touch data over fibre optics from the control room.

Air-core inductors
Ferrite-core inductors saturate in the scanner’s field, and nobody sells air-core parts at the values we need. I’m designing our own, sized with Wheeler’s formula.
Display driver board
I’m replacing the ferrous components on a commercial display’s driver board with non-magnetic parts.
Enclosure
A 3D-printed case for the display, electronics and battery, with no metal fasteners or framing.

Fourteen months at a nuclear plant

I supported reactor control instrumentation and Cobalt-60 medical isotope processing equipment. Most of the specifics are confidential, but here’s the short version.

Faulty temperature readings
Traced bad RTD readings in the Reactor Regulating System, a safety-critical control system, using plant trend data and system drawings. The replacement I recommended fixed them.
Stopped equipment
A failure in the Cobalt-60 processing equipment forced operators to do a critical step by hand, then halted processing. The original part was obsolete, so I found a qualified substitute and planned the replacement with maintenance.
Analysis tools
Built tools in Seeq to bring out plant conditions that weren’t directly visible in the available data.
In the field
More than 20 equipment walkdowns, over 10 of them inside the reactor building.

Fixing my headphones

I snapped the cable on my favourite wired headphones. Inside were three very thin conductors. I soldered them back together, designed and 3D printed a mold to keep the joints apart, filled it with resin, then sanded the result into a solid housing. They work again.
